<Layer kind="concept">

A data-centre GPU is a **package**: one or two compute dies in the middle and stacks of HBM (high-bandwidth memory) around them, on a silicon interposer or bridge that carries thousands of wires between them. Each HBM stack is DRAM dies stacked on a base die, with a 1,024-bit interface in HBM3 and HBM3E and a 2,048-bit one in HBM4 ("double from the previous generation", [SK hynix](https://news.skhynix.com/sk-hynix-completes-worlds-first-hbm4-development-and-readies-mass-production/)), which is much of why Rubin's per-stack bandwidth jumps; that width is why the stacks sit millimetres from the compute die instead of on the board.

The two packages in the animation:

- **H100 SXM5**: one die and <V of="gpu.h100.hbm_stacks" fmt="int" /> HBM3 stacks of <V of="pkg.h100.per_stack_bytes" fmt="bytes" />, <V of="pkg.h100.per_stack_bw" fmt="rate" /> each. The full GH100 die has six HBM sites; the SXM5 product uses five (NVIDIA's Hopper in-depth blog).
- **Blackwell Ultra**: two reticle-limited dies joined by a 10 TB/s die-to-die link into one GPU, and <V of="gpu.b300.hbm_stacks" fmt="int" /> twelve-high HBM3E stacks of <V of="pkg.b300.per_stack_bytes" fmt="bytes" />, <V of="pkg.b300.per_stack_bw" fmt="rate" /> each: <V of="gpu.b300.hbm_bytes" fmt="bytes" /> in all.

Capacity decides **how many GPUs** a model needs before any parallelism choice is made. Llama 3.1 405B in FP8 is <V of="fit.h100.llama3-405b.fp8.weights" fmt="bytes" /> of weights: <V of="fit.h100.llama3-405b.fp8.gpus" fmt="int" /> H100s, or <V of="fit.b300.llama3-405b.fp8.gpus" fmt="int" /> Blackwell Ultras.

Bandwidth decides **how fast** those weights can be used. Generating one token at batch size 1 reads every weight once, so a GPU holding <V of="fit.b300.llama3-405b.fp8.share" fmt="bytes" /> of them cannot produce a token faster than <V of="fit.b300.llama3-405b.fp8.read_time" fmt="time" />, whatever its arithmetic. Batching many requests shares that read; the [Serving site's roofline chapter](https://llm-inference-explained.vercel.app/learn/03-roofline) shows where it stops helping.

Arithmetic has grown faster than memory bandwidth. Dense FP8 per byte of HBM bandwidth (the roofline's ridge point) is <V of="pkg.h100.ridge_fp8" fmt="num" /> flop/byte on H100, <V of="pkg.b300.ridge_fp8" fmt="num" /> on Blackwell Ultra and <V of="pkg.rubin.ridge_fp8" fmt="num" /> on Rubin. And capacity has grown faster than bandwidth on Blackwell Ultra: reading its whole HBM once takes <V of="pkg.b300.sweep_time" fmt="time" />, against <V of="pkg.h100.sweep_time" fmt="time" /> on H100; Rubin's HBM4 brings it to <V of="pkg.rubin.sweep_time" fmt="time" />.

</Layer>

<Callout kind="illustrative">

Weights only: the KV cache, activations and the serving runtime also need HBM, so real deployments use more GPUs than this lower bound. FP4 is counted at half a byte per weight, without its block scales. The stack count is drawn only where NVIDIA states it (H100, Blackwell Ultra); for H200, Blackwell and Rubin the sources used give capacity and bandwidth but not the stack count.

</Callout>

<Layer kind="maths">

With $n$ stacks the package's capacity and bandwidth are $C = n\,c$ and $B = n\,b$ for one stack's $c$ and $b$. A model of $P$ parameters at $\beta$ bytes each has $W = \beta P$ bytes of weights, so

$$
N_{\text{GPU}} = \left\lceil \frac{\beta P}{C} \right\rceil, \qquad
t_{\text{read}} = \frac{\beta P / N_{\text{GPU}}}{B}, \qquad
\text{tokens/s at batch 1} \le \frac{1}{t_{\text{read}}} .
$$

The ridge point is $F / B$ for dense peak $F$: a kernel doing fewer than $F/B$ operations per byte it reads from HBM waits on memory.

</Layer>

```ts
const wb = weightsBytes(mid, fmt);
const n = Math.ceil(wb / g.hbm_bytes);
const share = wb / n;
return {
  weights: wb,
  gpus: n,
  share,
  fill: share / g.hbm_bytes,
  read_time: share / g.hbm_bw,
  tokens_per_s: g.hbm_bw / share,
};
```

Blackwell's two dies are each as large as the lithography reticle allows; joining two of them is how the GPU grew past one die's limit. Rubin's specifications come from NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72 page, whose NVLink figures NVIDIA marks preliminary.
