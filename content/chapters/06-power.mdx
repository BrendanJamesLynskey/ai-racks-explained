A GB200 NVL72 rack draws about <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.rack_power_w" fmt="watts" />, NVIDIA's own figure in the DGX GB200 user guide. Spread over its <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.gpus" fmt="int" /> GPUs that is <V of="pow.per_gpu_w" fmt="watts" /> each, against a Blackwell GPU's maximum of <V of="gpu.b200.tdp_w" fmt="watts" />: the rest goes to the Grace CPUs, the NVLink switch trays, network cards, fans and conversion losses.

<span id="r-shelves" />

**Power shelves.** The rack does not take mains power into each tray. <V of="pow.shelves" fmt="int" /> power shelves, each holding <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.psus_per_shelf" fmt="int" /> supplies of <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.psu_w" fmt="watts" />, convert AC to a DC bus of about <V of="pow.dc_v" fmt="int" /> V. That is <V of="pow.installed_w" fmt="watts" /> of supplies, arranged N+N: either half can carry the rack, so <V of="pow.usable_w" fmt="watts" /> survives the loss of half of them, <V of="pow.margin" fmt="num" /> times the rack's draw.

<span id="r-busbar" />

**The busbar.** At <V of="pow.dc_v" fmt="int" /> V, <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.rack_power_w" fmt="watts" /> is <V of="pow.busbar_a" fmt="int" /> A, carried by copper bars down the back of the rack. Current is what costs copper and heat: the loss in a conductor is I²R, so for the same power it falls with the square of the voltage. NVIDIA's plan for racks of <V of="fac.power.mw_rack_w" fmt="watts" /> (stated for 2027, so planned, not shipping) is to bring <V of="fac.power.hvdc_v" fmt="int" /> V DC to the rack instead of <V of="fac.power.busbar_v" fmt="int" /> V: it says a 1 MW rack at 54 V would need up to <V of="fac.power.copper_1mw_kg" fmt="int" /> kg of busbar copper.

The copper column is our extrapolation, not NVIDIA's: copper in proportion to current, at the current density of NVIDIA's 1 MW, 54 V example.

<span id="r-smoothing" />

**Power smoothing.** Training is synchronous. Every GPU computes, then every GPU waits for the same all-reduce, so a whole hall's power rises and falls together; the Llama 3 paper reports fluctuations "on the order of tens of megawatts". The animation shows one rack's share. With no storage, the grid sees the full swing. GB300 NVL72's power supplies hold <V of="fac.power.storage_j_per_gpu" fmt="int" /> J per GPU in capacitors (NVIDIA's figure). If the load swings every 0.05 s, that store flattens the grid's draw completely and cuts the peak by <V of="smooth.fast.gb300.peak_cut" fmt="pct" />. If it swings every quarter of a second, the store runs empty in each high phase and the cut falls to <V of="smooth.mid.gb300.peak_cut" fmt="pct" />. At one second it is <V of="smooth.slow.gb300.peak_cut" fmt="pct" />. Capacitors are for sub-second ripple; slower swings need the other tools NVIDIA describes (power caps that ramp gradually, and a "power burner" that keeps GPUs drawing while the job pauses) or batteries outside the rack. NVIDIA reports a 30% cut in peak grid demand on a real training run; that is its measurement, quoted, not this model's output.

**Tokens per megawatt.** Power is now the scarce input for many AI data centres, so serving efficiency is often quoted per megawatt. Chapter 8's simulated rack produces <V of="dis.12-6.nvlink.gb200.tok_s_per_mw" fmt="int" /> output tokens a second per megawatt of GPU power, using the simulator's illustrative power coefficients; [chapter 11](/learn/11-cost) puts the whole rack's power and a facility overhead on top. Vendors quote this metric too: NVIDIA says Vera Rubin NVL72 "delivers up to 10x more tokens per megawatt than NVIDIA GB200 NVL72", a claim we attribute and do not model.

<Layer kind="maths">

A rack alternates between $P_{\text{hi}}$ for $t_{\text{hi}}$ and $P_{\text{lo}}$ for $t_{\text{lo}}$, with stored energy $E$. Holding the grid flat at $P_g$ through the high phase takes $(P_{\text{hi}} - P_g)\,t_{\text{hi}} \le E$ from the store, so the lowest flat level is

$$
P_g = \max\!\left(\bar P,\; P_{\text{hi}} - \frac{E}{t_{\text{hi}}}\right),
\qquad
P_{\text{low phase}} = P_{\text{lo}} + \frac{(P_{\text{hi}} - P_g)\,t_{\text{hi}}}{t_{\text{lo}}},
$$

where the second is the draw that refills the store in the low phase. When $E \ge (P_{\text{hi}} - \bar P)\,t_{\text{hi}}$ both levels equal the average $\bar P$: the grid sees no swing at all.

On the bus, current and resistive loss for power $P$ at voltage $V$ are

$$
I = \frac{P}{V}, \qquad P_{\text{loss}} = I^2 R \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{P_{\text{loss}}(V)}{P_{\text{loss}}(54\ \text{V})} = \left(\frac{54}{V}\right)^{2}.
$$

</Layer>

<Layer kind="code">

The smoothing model, cut from [`src/lib/racks/model.ts`](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/ai-racks-explained/blob/main/src/lib/racks/model.ts):

```ts
const period = tHi + tLo;
const pAvg = (pHi * tHi + pLo * tLo) / period;
const level = Math.max(pAvg, pHi - eStore / tHi);
const used = (pHi - level) * tHi;
const low = pLo + used / tLo;
```
