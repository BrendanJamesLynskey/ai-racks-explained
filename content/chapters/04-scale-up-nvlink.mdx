    tex="T_{\text{ring}} = \htmlClass{hl-rs}{\frac{n-1}{n}\,\frac{S}{B}} + \htmlClass{hl-ag}{\frac{n-1}{n}\,\frac{S}{B}} = \frac{2(n-1)}{n}\,\frac{S}{\htmlClass{hl-nvl}{B}}"

<Layer kind="concept">

Many of the communications in a model split over GPUs are **collectives**: every GPU contributes and every GPU receives. Tensor parallelism ends each attention and MLP block with an **all-reduce** (every GPU needs the sum of everyone's partial results); expert parallelism routes tokens to the GPUs holding their experts with an **all-to-all**.

The animation runs the classic ring all-reduce on real numbers. Each GPU cuts its vector into $n$ chunks. In the **reduce-scatter** half, every GPU sends one chunk to its neighbour, which adds it to its own; after $n-1$ steps each GPU owns one complete chunk of the sum. In the **all-gather** half, the finished chunks travel round the ring for another $n-1$ steps. Every link is busy every step, and each GPU sends $2(n-1)/n$ of its vector, close to twice its size, whatever $n$ is: the ring is bandwidth-optimal (Patarasuk and Yuan, 2009).

So the time is set by the link. Over NVLink, at <V of="tp.n8.64MB.nvlink_bw" fmt="rate" /> per direction, a 64 MB all-reduce over 8 GPUs takes <V of="tp.n8.64MB.t_nvlink" fmt="time" />; over the GPU's <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.nic_bps" fmt="bits" /> network port it takes <V of="tp.n8.64MB.t_nic" fmt="time" />, <V of="tp.n8.64MB.ratio" fmt="num" /> times longer. A tensor-parallel group does this twice per layer, every layer, so it is kept inside the NVLink domain.

NVLink switches can also do the reduction themselves: NVIDIA's switches since the third-generation NVSwitch add the vectors in the network (SHARP in-network reduction, with multicast), so each GPU sends its vector once and receives the sum once, <V of="tp.n8.64MB.t_switch" fmt="time" /> for the same 64 MB.

</Layer>

## Small messages: the latency term

<AlphaBetaWidget>
  <Eq
    label="With a fixed cost alpha per step, the ring all-reduce takes two times n minus 1 alphas plus two times n minus 1 over n times S over B; the in-switch reduction takes one alpha plus S over B"
    tex="\begin{gathered} T_{\text{ring}} = \htmlClass{hl-rs}{2(n-1)\,\alpha} + \frac{2(n-1)}{n}\,\frac{S}{\htmlClass{hl-nvl}{B}} \\ T_{\text{in-switch}} = \alpha_{\text{s}} + \frac{S}{B} \end{gathered}"
  />
</AlphaBetaWidget>

<Layer kind="concept">

The ring above is timed by bandwidth alone, which is right for the gigabytes of a training step's gradients and wrong for the messages a decode step sends. Every step of a collective also pays a fixed cost: launching the transfer, synchronising with the neighbour, the trip through the switch. Call it $\alpha$. The ring has $2(n-1)$ steps, so it pays $2(n-1)\alpha$ before a single byte's bandwidth counts; the in-switch reduction pays one fixed cost $\alpha_{\text{s}}$.

The model takes $\alpha$ from measurement. On an eight-GPU NVLink 4 node, NCCL's all-reduce of small messages takes about <V of="ab.ringSmall" fmt="time" /> as a ring and <V of="ab.nvlsSmall" fmt="time" /> through the switches (NVLS), for anything up to 64 KiB ([Ma et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05951), section VII-C). Read as $2(n-1)\alpha$ that is <V of="ab.alphaRing" fmt="time" /> per ring step. NVIDIA does not publish NVLink 5's latency, so the B200 curve uses the same $\alpha$: an assumption, labelled in the widget.

Now a tensor-parallel decode step. Llama 3 70B's all-reduce at batch 8 is <V of="ab.dec8.size" fmt="bytes" /> (8 tokens of <V of="ab.d70" fmt="int" /> 16-bit values). On eight B200s it takes <V of="ab.dec8.ring.t" fmt="time" /> as a ring, of which <V of="ab.dec8.ring.latShare" fmt="pct" /> is the fixed cost; the bandwidth-only formula says <V of="ab.dec8.bwOnly" fmt="time" />, <V of="ab.dec8under" fmt="num" /> times too little. Below the crossover, <V of="ab.dec8.ring.crossover" fmt="bytes" /> for a ring of eight, doubling NVLink's bandwidth hardly helps; cutting the steps does. That is why the in-switch reduction and one-shot algorithms (every GPU reads everyone's data directly in one step) matter for decode, and why tensor parallelism stays inside the NVLink domain: the network adds its own latency as well as less bandwidth.

**A measured check.** For large messages the same paper measures NCCL's NVLS all-reduce at <V of="ab.nvlsBig" fmt="rate" /> on that node. The model's in-switch bound for a gigabyte there is <V of="val.nccl-nvls-h200.model" fmt="rate" />, so the bound is <V of="val.nccl-nvls-h200.ratio" fmt="num" /> times what NCCL reaches. The paper does not say whether its figure is NCCL's algorithm or bus bandwidth; if it were bus bandwidth, NCCL would reach less of the bound still. This is the site's first check of the model against a measurement rather than against the vendor's own arithmetic ([the checks](/about#validation)).

</Layer>

## The NVL72 rack as one domain

<Nvl72Diagram />

An NVL72 rack wires all <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.gpus" fmt="int" /> GPUs together through <V of="nvl.chips" fmt="int" /> switch chips. Each GPU has <V of="gpu.b200.nvlink_links" fmt="int" /> NVLinks and each switch chip has <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.ports_per_switch_chip" fmt="int" /> ports, so the GPUs' <V of="nvl.links" fmt="int" /> links fill the chips' <V of="nvl.ports" fmt="int" /> ports exactly, one link from every GPU to every chip. Every pair of GPUs is one switch hop apart, at the full <V of="nvl.per_gpu_bidir" fmt="rate" /> per GPU (both directions), <V of="nvl.total_bidir" fmt="rate" /> across the rack (NVIDIA rounds it to <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.nvlink_total" fmt="rate" />). This is what NVIDIA means by the rack acting as one GPU: for collectives, any 72-GPU group is as close as two GPUs on one tray.

It matters most for **all-to-all**. An expert-parallel layer across all <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.gpus" fmt="int" /> GPUs sends most of each GPU's tokens to other GPUs; 64 MB per GPU takes <V of="a2a.64MB.nvlink" fmt="time" /> inside the rack, and would take <V of="a2a.64MB.net" fmt="time" /> over the network ports.

<Callout kind="illustrative">

The ring and all-to-all times in this section are bandwidth terms only (the latency term is above, for all-reduce): no protocol overhead, perfect overlap of sends. NVLink figures are NVIDIA's bidirectional totals halved. The in-switch reduction is modelled as each GPU sending and receiving the vector once; how close real collectives come to that depends on the library and the message size.

</Callout>

<Layer kind="maths">

With $n$ GPUs, vector size $S$ and link bandwidth $B$ per direction, the ring sends $n-1$ chunks of $S/n$ in each half:

$$
T_{\text{ring}} = 2\,\frac{n-1}{n}\,\frac{S}{B}, \qquad
T_{\text{in-switch}} = \frac{S}{B}, \qquad
T_{\text{all-to-all}} = \frac{n-1}{n}\,\frac{S}{B}.
$$

The NVL72 wiring: $72 \times 18 = 1296$ GPU links and $18 \times 72 = 1296$ switch ports. A switch tray's two chips carry $2 \times 72 \times 50\ \text{GB/s} \times 8 =$ <V of="nvl.tray_bits_dir" fmt="bits" /> per direction, which is the figure in NVIDIA's DGX GB200 user guide.

</Layer>
