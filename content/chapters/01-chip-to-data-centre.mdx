<Layer kind="concept">

A training or inference cluster is built in rungs, each a box of the rung below. The animation climbs them for **GB200 NVL72** racks, arranged the way NVIDIA's published DGX SuperPOD reference architecture arranges them:

- **The package**: one GPU, two compute dies and their HBM, reading memory at <V of="ladder.package.bw" fmt="rate" />.
- **The compute tray**: <V of="ladder.tray.gpus" fmt="int" /> GPUs and two Grace CPUs on one 1U tray.
- **The rack**: <V of="ladder.rack.children" fmt="int" /> compute trays and <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.switch_trays" fmt="int" /> NVLink switch trays. All <V of="ladder.rack.gpus" fmt="int" /> GPUs share one NVLink domain, so a GPU reaches any other in the rack at <V of="ladder.rack.bw" fmt="rate" /> per direction, exactly as fast as it reaches its neighbour on the same tray.
- **The scalable unit**: <V of="ladder.su.children" fmt="int" /> racks, <V of="ladder.su.gpus" fmt="int" /> GPUs, joined by an InfiniBand fabric. Each GPU has one <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.nic_bps" fmt="bits" /> network port: <V of="ladder.su.bw" fmt="rate" /> per direction.
- **The SuperPOD**: up to <V of="ladder.pod.children" fmt="int" /> scalable units, <V of="ladder.pod.gpus" fmt="int" /> GPUs.
- **The hall**: sized by power. At about <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.rack_power_w" fmt="watts" /> per rack, 100 MW of rack power is <V of="ladder.hall.children" fmt="int" /> racks, <V of="ladder.hall.gpus" fmt="int" /> GPUs.

Watch the bars as the camera zooms out. The rack boundary is the cliff: crossing it, a GPU's bandwidth falls <V of="ladder.su.drop" fmt="num" /> times, and beyond it, it never recovers. One gigabyte takes <V of="ladder.package.time" fmt="time" /> to read from HBM, <V of="ladder.rack.time" fmt="time" /> to send over NVLink and <V of="ladder.su.time" fmt="time" /> to send over the network.

That one ratio shapes the rest of the design. Traffic that is heavy and frequent (tensor-parallel all-reduces in every layer, expert-parallel all-to-alls, KV caches handed from prefill to decode) is kept inside the NVLink domain. Traffic that is lighter or can overlap with compute (data-parallel gradient averaging, pipeline-stage activations) crosses the network. The rest of this site follows that line: what is inside the package ([chapter 2](/learn/02-the-package)), on the tray ([chapter 3](/learn/03-trays-and-nodes)), in the rack ([chapter 4](/learn/04-scale-up-nvlink)) and between racks ([chapter 5](/learn/05-scale-out-network)).

</Layer>

<Callout kind="illustrative">

The bars are peak bandwidths from NVIDIA's published figures; achieved rates are lower and latency is not modelled. HBM is a read (or write) rate; NVLink is NVIDIA's bidirectional figure halved; the network figure is one ConnectX-7 port per GPU, as in the DGX GB200 tray (GB300 NVL72 doubles it to <V of="sys.gb300-nvl72.nic_bps" fmt="bits" /> per GPU). The hall rung is an illustrative size: rack power only, with no cooling, networking or facility overhead. Every figure and its source: [the specifications](/specs).

</Callout>

<Layer kind="maths">

At rung $r$ with $N_r$ GPUs and bandwidth $B_r$ per GPU per direction, moving $S$ bytes out of one GPU takes, as a bandwidth term only,

$$
t_r = \frac{S}{B_r}, \qquad \text{the rung's aggregate is } N_r B_r, \qquad \text{the drop from the rung below is } \frac{B_{r-1}}{B_r}.
$$

For GB200 the per-GPU figures are $B_{\text{HBM}} =$ <V of="gpu.b200.hbm_bw" fmt="rate" />, $B_{\text{NVLink}} = \tfrac12 \times$ <V of="gpu.b200.nvlink_bidir" fmt="rate" />, and $B_{\text{network}} =$ <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.nic_bps" fmt="bits" /> $\div 8$. The rack's power per GPU is the rack's power divided by its <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.gpus" fmt="int" /> GPUs, so the power of a rung is $N_r \times P_{\text{rack}} / 72$; the hall holds $\lfloor 100\ \text{MW} / P_{\text{rack}} \rfloor$ racks.

</Layer>

<Layer kind="code">

The rungs' derived figures, cut from [`src/lib/racks/model.ts`](https://github.com/BrendanJamesLynskey/ai-racks-explained/blob/main/src/lib/racks/model.ts) (the Python reference, `reference/rack_model.py`, is the same, and the unit tests require identical results):

```ts
return rungs.map((r, i) => ({
  ...r,
  i,
  time: payload / r.bw,
  power_w: r.gpus * perGpuW,
  aggregate: r.gpus * r.bw,
  drop: i > 0 ? rungs[i - 1]!.bw / r.bw : 1.0,
}));
```
