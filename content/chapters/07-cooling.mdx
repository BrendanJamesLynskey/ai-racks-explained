<Layer kind="concept">

Every watt a rack draws ends up as heat, and something has to carry it away. A fluid carries heat $Q = \dot m\,c_p\,\Delta T$: its mass flow, times its specific heat, times how much it warms. Water's specific heat is <V of="phys.water_cp" fmt="int" /> J/(kg K) (NIST's equation at 25 °C); air's is <V of="phys.air_cp" fmt="int" /> J/(kg K), and a cubic metre of air weighs only <V of="phys.air_density" fmt="num" /> kg. So, volume for volume, water carries thousands of times more heat.

<span id="r-air" />

**Why air runs out.** To carry a <V of="sys.gb200-nvl72.rack_power_w" fmt="watts" /> rack's heat, warming by 15 K, air needs <V of="flow.r120.air_m3_s" fmt="num" /> m³ a second (<V of="flow.r120.air_cfm" fmt="int" /> cubic feet a minute) through one rack. Water warming by 10 K needs <V of="flow.r120.water_l_min" fmt="int" /> litres a minute: <V of="flow.r120.vol_ratio" fmt="int" /> times less volume. NVIDIA's own comparison is that data centres "once operated at <V of="fac.cooling.legacy_rack_w" fmt="watts" /> per rack" and that hyperscale facilities now support "over <V of="fac.cooling.hyperscale_rack_w" fmt="watts" /> per rack"; air was designed for the first.

<span id="r-loop" />

**The liquid loop.** In a GB200 NVL72, the CPUs and GPUs sit under cold plates, and coolant runs up and down the rack through manifolds; networking and storage stay air-cooled (NVIDIA's DGX GB200 user guide). The animation follows the coolant:

- the **coolant distribution unit (CDU)** sends it out a few kelvin above the facility water that cools it (the _approach_; <V of="fac.cooling.cdu_approach_k" fmt="int" /> K for the partner CDU NVIDIA cites, rated at <V of="fac.cooling.cdu_w" fmt="watts" />);
- the **supply manifold** feeds the compute trays in parallel;
- the **cold plates** take the heat, warming the coolant by $\Delta T$;
- the **return manifold** brings it back to the CDU, whose exchanger hands the heat to the **facility water**, which leaves for chillers, cooling towers or dry coolers.

With 90% of the heat on cold plates (an illustrative share: NVIDIA does not publish one), facility water at 30 °C and a 10 K rise, the loop runs at <V of="loop.t30.d10.l_min" fmt="int" /> L/min, <V of="loop.t30.d10.per_tray_kg_s" fmt="num" /> kg/s through each tray, from <V of="loop.t30.d10.t_sup" fmt="temp" /> to <V of="loop.t30.d10.t_ret" fmt="temp" />. One <V of="fac.cooling.cdu_w" fmt="watts" /> CDU serves <V of="loop.t30.d10.racks_per_cdu" fmt="int" /> such racks.

<span id="r-warm" />

**Warm water.** The warmer the coolant can run, the less work the facility does to cool it: with warm enough facility water, dry coolers that use outside air can replace chillers. NVIDIA's 100 MW Vera Rubin reference factory states a <V of="sys.vr-nvl72.inlet_c" fmt="temp" /> inlet temperature. Try 40 °C facility water in the animation: the coolant then leaves the CDU at <V of="loop.t40.d10.t_sup" fmt="temp" />. The limit is the chips: the cold plate has to keep each die below its own maximum while the coolant arrives warmer.

**One megawatt.** At <V of="fac.power.mw_rack_w" fmt="watts" /> (NVIDIA's planned Kyber-class rack), a 10 K rise needs <V of="flow.r1000.water_l_min" fmt="int" /> L/min of water, and air would need <V of="flow.r1000.air_m3_s" fmt="int" /> m³ a second: liquid is not optional.

</Layer>

<Layer kind="maths">

The coolant's mass flow, and the volume of air that would carry the same heat:

$$
\dot m = \frac{Q_{\text{liquid}}}{c_{p,\text{water}}\,\Delta T_{\text{water}}}, \qquad
\dot V_{\text{air}} = \frac{Q}{\rho_{\text{air}}\,c_{p,\text{air}}\,\Delta T_{\text{air}}}.
$$

Water's $c_p$ comes from NIST's Shomate equation for liquid water at $T = 298.15$ K ($t = T/1000$, coefficients $A \ldots E$, molar mass $M$):

$$
c_p = \frac{A + Bt + Ct^2 + Dt^3 + E/t^2}{M}, \qquad
c_{p,\text{air}} = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1}
$$

with NASA's standard-day $\gamma =$ <V of="phys.air_gamma" fmt="num" /> and $R =$ <V of="phys.air_r" fmt="int" /> J/(kg K). The coolant leaves the CDU at the facility temperature plus the approach, and returns $\Delta T$ warmer.

</Layer>

<Remember>

- Every watt a rack draws becomes heat: $Q = \dot m\,c_p\,\Delta T$.
- Volume for volume, water carries thousands of times more heat than air.
- The liquid loop: CDU, supply manifold, cold plates, return manifold, facility water.
- Warmer coolant lets dry coolers replace chillers; at a megawatt a rack, liquid is not optional.

<Keep>
  $\dot m = Q / (c_p\,\Delta T)$: halve the temperature rise, double the flow.
